# Doomscrolling PSA: concepts considered

Ten directions, all riffing on 90s (and turn-of-the-century) anti-smoking and anti-drug spots.

1. **This is your brain on the feed.** The frying-pan ad: "This is your brain. This is the feed." A thumb flicks and the egg fries. Instantly recognizable, but it's one visual gag and the original is anti-drug, not anti-smoking.
2. **Not even once.** A late-night bedroom, an alarm clock that keeps jumping forward, and a narrator who keeps hearing "just one more." Ends on a stark black card: DOOMSCROLLING. NOT EVEN ONCE. *(chosen)*
3. **Body bags, but hours.** The truth-campaign stunt: activists dump a pile of hourglasses outside a feed company's HQ. "Every night, the average scroller loses..." Strong, but it leans on statistics we'd have to invent.
4. **The testimonial.** A Debi-style close-up of a sufferer with a permanently curled "scroll thumb" and a stoma-style ring light. Funny, but it mocks a real ad about real cancer patients.
5. **The guy in the trenchcoat.** "Hey kid, wanna see what's trending?" A shady figure opens his coat to reveal phones. Classic peer-pressure film; a bit thin past the first joke.
6. **Warning labels.** Surgeon-General-style boxes on everything: "WARNING: Doomscrolling is a leading cause of 3 A.M." Great as a beat, too static as a whole spot.
7. **The hearing.** A 1994 tobacco-CEO hearing parody: feed executives swearing "I believe the feed is not addictive." Sharp, but it needs several convincing actors' worth of voices.
8. **Only you can prevent doomscrolling.** A McGruff/Smokey-style mascot. Charming, but tonally softer than the request.
9. **Like, subscribe, and close the app.** The ironic end screen: the narrator asks for the like and the subscribe, then the last button is "close the app," and the TV powers off. Works best as a tag on another concept. *(used as the ending)*
10. **The jar of attention.** The tar-in-a-jar demo: a lab tech squeezes a year of scrolling out of a sponge into a jar. Visually gross in the right way, but hard to make "attention" physical without explaining it.

## Why #2 with #6 and #9 folded in

"Not even once" carries a full arc (first look, escalation, morning after, slogan) in 60 seconds, and the alarm clock gives a visual for time disappearing without any invented statistics. The warning labels (#6) work as a beat in the middle, a "somebody built it" blueprint adds the industry angle from #7 without needing a cast, and #9 is the best last line: the PSA itself asks for engagement and then tells you to leave.
